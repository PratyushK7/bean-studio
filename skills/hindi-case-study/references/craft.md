# Craft: how the hits make a viewer feel, wonder and stay

Study of 2026-11-12. 45 transcripts: 40 hits (253K to 12M views) and 5 flops (468 to 8.4K) from the same channels, read as a
writer: Haldiram's (Shivanshu Agrawal, 12M), the dhaba (Money Code, 2.2M on a channel with a 13K median), the sleeper bus
(HiddenOps, 3.8M) and Tukaram Mundhe (HiddenOps, 4.7M) in full or near full, the opening and closing minutes of 20 more, every
re-hook-like line of all 40, and the 5 flops against them. Raw notes: `C:\Users\praty\youtube\channels\hindi\research\script-study-notes.md`.
The quotes below are for studying the mechanism. Never reuse their lines, their characters' names or their scenes.

## 1. What separates a hit from a flat script

- **Counting does not.** Per-minute direct address, questions, लेकिन, open-loop phrases, emotion words, stakes words, numbers and
  sentence length are the same in hits and flops (medians: but 2.3 vs 1.8, open loops 0.1 vs 0.1, emotion words 0.6 vs 0.7,
  numbers 6.6 vs 6.4). A script can tick every box and be dead. Our first jewellery script did exactly that.
- **Craft does not rescue a weak topic.** HiddenOps' Rapido film (5.8K) uses the sleeper-bus formula beat for beat (an emergency
  cold open at 1:20 am, "80, 50 या उससे भी कम?", "जवाब कहानी के आखिर में"); the oil-heist tunnel (468) and Toyota-Honda (8.4K) open
  like movies. The topic and the packaging decide the click; the script decides whether they stay to the end. Write as if the
  topic were already good, and do not promise the user that a script fixes a weak one.
- **What the hits have, and our flat script did not**: the information is *withheld on purpose* and the viewer is made to *feel*
  something about a person while they wait. Every hit is built on the same six parts (section 2). The devices people quote
  (re-hooks, open loops) are only the visible surface of those parts; on their own they sound like filler.

## 2. The engine: six parts every hit has

1. **One question the viewer cannot drop, with a surprising exact answer.** It is asked in the hook as a guess with wrong options
   ("₹1200 में मालिक का कितना? 500, 300, 100?", "पूरा या फिर आधा? दोनों ही गलत"), carried the whole way, and answered with the
   exact number at the end. In a story video the question is about a person ("क्या सब जिंदा उतरे?", "ऐसा क्या हुआ कि...").
2. **A person to follow, built from the viewer's own life.** A want in their own words, a pain the viewer knows ("बॉस रोज डांटता था
   और सैलरी हर महीने 25 तारीख को खुद ही गायब हो जाती थी"; a driver docked ₹2,000 and mocked in public), a naive belief that is the
   viewer's belief ("खाना सबको चाहिए, नुकसान कहां से होगा"), and a mistake we see coming. In the explainer shape the person is
   *you* ("आप... ₹1,200 का टिकट लिया... और आपको लगता है यह बस वाला तो मालामाल होगा"). A person with no pain, no flaw and no loss
   (our Ramesh, who only sat on his gaddi) is a narrator's puppet and nobody cares what happens to him.
3. **Stakes made physical, usually the family's.** 18 years of savings, Babuji's land (he touches the soil to his forehead at the
   registry), his wife's mother's bangles ("यह बुरे वक्त में काम आएंगे" / "तो इसे बुरा वक्त बनने भी मत देना"), a friend who signs as
   guarantor ("डूबा तो हम दोनों डूबेंगे"), house papers in the lender's safe. Rupees alone are not stakes; what the rupees *are* is.
4. **Opposition with a face.** Every cost is a character who walks in and takes a share: "इसी दफ्तर में 1200 के टिकट का पहला
   हिस्सेदार पैदा हुआ", "यही है टिकट का सबसे बड़ा हिस्सेदार: पंप", "फिर बाकी हिस्सेदार भी कतार में आ खड़े हुए". Plus a rival or a
   villain (the seth who mocked him, the gutkha syndicate that calls with a threat, HLL's secret mission against Nirma, the glass
   showroom across the road) and the system (permits, the duty, the bank: "बस रुक सकती है, किस्त नहीं रुकती").
5. **Reversals.** The line goes up and down, never flat: Haldiram's family split leaves him "रातों-रात सड़क पे" with "एक फूटी कौड़ी
   तक नहीं", a childhood friend calls his name in the market, he climbs back; the 1984 riots burn the Delhi shop to zero, he rebuilds.
   Raju's first month looks great, then the profit is ₹7,000 ("अरे इससे ज्यादा तो नौकरी में मिलता था"); the mentor turns it; then
   the rains shut him for two weeks. A rise with no fall is a brochure.
6. **Plants and payoffs.** Something small is put down early and picked up late, and the viewer feels the click: the ₹5 item that
   is "ढाबे वाले की असली सोने की खान" (paid off 9 minutes later: पापड़ और छाछ); the old ticket with the dream math on its back (falls
   out of the red diary in the last minute); the lender's "बस का क्या है, कल को जल भी सकती है" (the fire); the seth's "तू ड्राइवर,
   मैं सेठ" (the end proves the driver earned more than the owner). The hook's question is the biggest plant of all.

## 3. Story shapes (beat skeletons, as % of runtime)

**A. Economics told as an owner's story** (sleeper bus 3.8M, dhaba 2.2M; the strongest shape for "economics of owning X").
0-3% cold open at the disaster or the paradox, with the money question as a guess and "जवाब आखिर में" · 3-4% straight on with the story (HiddenOps stops here for a fiction disclaimer; we never do) · 4-10% the person: pain,
humiliation or a dead-end job, the naive math ("आप भी यही सोच रहे हैं ना? याद रखिए, यह हिसाब आखिर में पलटेगा") · 10-25% the leap:
stakes laid on the table one sacrifice at a time, the first share-takers born in scenes (the lender, the seller), a temptation ·
25-60% running it: each cost arrives as an event (a tyre bursts, the pump, the agent's cut, the permit officer), a false high, the
low point, a mentor or rival with a riddle line, a fix that works · 60-75% the cold open catches up (the disaster happens in
sequence) and its aftermath · 75-92% recovery, a second crisis, the rule learned · last 5% the answer as a staged countdown
("सबसे पहले पंप 517, बचे 683..."), an irony that calls back the wound, one last upward turn, a final image.

**A-explainer. "You" are the owner or the customer** (HinExp bus 2.1M, petrol pump 1.1M; Paisa Decode turf 1.1M). No characters;
the viewer is the protagonist. "एक सीन इमेजिन करो... आप..." · the belief in the viewer's voice with naive math · "भाई आज मैं आपका यह
बबल फोड़ने आया हूं" · a roadmap of secrets ("वो ऊपर की कमाई जिसकी चर्चा कोई नहीं करता") · each chapter is one monster with a
personality ("सबसे बड़ा राक्षस: डीजल"; "एक बस जो खड़ी है वह एक घाव है जो हर दिन गहरा होता जाता है") · the hidden income · the
risks · the verdict and the observation test. Works when the voice has strong attitude and images; flat without them.

**B. A company's rise and fall** (Shivanshu: Haldiram's 12M, Byju's 10M, Nirma 9.2M, Oberoi 8.6M). Short hook (20-40 s: year,
place, a humble act or the peak, dramatic irony or "ऐसा क्या हुआ कि...", "यह कहानी है X की") · birth, family, the first want ·
each win as a named strategy with its mechanism and one number, told inside the scene (naming the sev after the Maharaja =
"ब्रांडिंग जीनियस") · "सब कुछ बहुत अच्छा चल रहा था कि तभी..." before every fall · the rival's counter-move as its own scene · the
fall, with what they should have done and "लेकिन उन्होंने इनमें से कुछ भी नहीं किया" · then vs now ("दो पैसों में... आज 9,000
करोड़") · one-line moral. Fast (175-190 words a minute), plain, almost no questions: the events carry it.

**C. A person's story as a thriller** (Tukaram 4.7M, pilots 4.3M, Rolls-Royce 1.3M, Mukesh vs Anil 1.5M). Cold open on a stamped
moment of humiliation, peak or collapse, with one line of dialogue ("ए हिंदुस्तानी, तेरी हैसियत भी है इस कार को छूने की?") · the
size of what is at stake ("एक लॉबी 250 करोड़ इकट्ठा कर रही है") tied to the viewer ("आपकी थाली में और आपके बच्चे के गिलास में") ·
an ESCALATION LADDER: every rung a bigger enemy, a bigger risk to the hero, a bigger reveal ("लेकिन असली धमाका अब हुआ") · the
threat ("मुंडे साहब बहुत तेज दौड़ रहे हैं आप... घर परिवार का ख्याल रखिए") and the hero's answer · each rung ends on a moral
one-liner ("समस्या एनालॉग पनीर नहीं थी। समस्या झूठ थी।") · back to the cold open at the end for the last image.

**Mystery: "X is in trouble"** (DMart 3.3M and 1.9M, Patanjali 1.5M). The paradox in the first sentence ("पार्किंग फुल... उसी शाम
दलाल स्ट्रीट की स्क्रीन पर उसका नाम लाल है") · the hidden clue ("जवाब उसमें छुपे एक छोटे से नंबर में था। ज्यादातर लोगों ने उसे
पढ़ा तक नहीं। जिन्होंने पढ़ा उन्होंने अगली सुबह बेचना शुरू कर दिया।") · the backstory of the strength that becomes the weakness ·
the enemy · the open verdict · the observation test in the viewer's next visit.

**List** (7 shop tricks, 1.1M). Each item escalates in intimacy or danger, and is aimed at the viewer's own mind: "पहली और सबसे
ताकतवर", "शायद सबसे चालाक", "जिसे बहुत कम लोग पकड़ पाते हैं", "जो सीधे हमारे डर पर वार करती है". Each item is a mini-story
(two salons face to face), never a definition.

**Compare** (rent vs buy, 1.1M): two people with the same salary on two roads, checked at the same ages, one exact result at the end.

## 4. Hooks: eight shapes that hit (each real)

a) **Cold open at the peak or the fall**, time and place in sentence one, a person, a line of dialogue, then a jump back: Mallya's
   jet at 11:30 pm "उसके चेहरे पर मुस्कान थी"; Anil Ambani in a black Mercedes as the lawyer calls ("4 हफ्ते... वरना 3 महीने जेल...
   अचानक Mercedes के अंदर सन्नाटा"); "रात के 2:40, घाट की ढलान... उनके नीचे आग जाग चुकी थी।"
b) **Humble act plus dramatic irony**: "साल 1919, बीकानेर... एक 12 साल का बच्चा... दो पैसे में... इस बात से बिल्कुल अनजान कि..."
c) **The paradox scene**: two identical salons face to face, one always waiting, the other barber reading the paper; five dhabas,
   four empty; the dusty cement shop whose lala owns the biggest plot in town; a shop that sells cheaper, delivers free, and its
   owner still gets richer: "क्या यह मुमकिन है? नहीं। लेकिन..."
d) **The viewer's own memory**: "आपके पापा ने या आपके ताऊ जी ने जिंदगी में एक बाइक जरूर चलाई होगी... भरो, बंद करो, भूल जाओ...
   और फिर 2010 में यह जोड़ी अचानक टूट गई।"
e) **The betrayal charge**: "यही अमेरिकी कंपनी भारतीयों के साथ एक ऐसा धोखा कर रही थी कि अगर आज कोई करे तो सीधा जेल की हवा खाए।"
f) **The money guess plus personal stakes plus one thing**: "पूरा या फिर आधा? दोनों ही गलत... हो सकता है अगला टर्फ तुम खोलो अपनी
   सेविंग से... उसके बाद मैं तुम्हें वो एक चीज बताऊंगा जो किराए की जमीन पे भी टर्फ को बचा लेती है।"
g) **The hidden number**: "जवाब पूरी रिपोर्ट में नहीं था। जवाब उसमें छुपे एक छोटे से नंबर में था।"
h) **A big fight made personal**: "जिसका असर सिर्फ अडानी और बिड़ला पर नहीं, बल्कि आपके घर की दीवारों तक पड़ सकता है।"

**The gain.** The winners also tell the viewer what they will be able to do: the turf video says "हो सकता है अगला टर्फ तुम खोलो अपनी
सेविंग से... मैं तुम्हें वो एक चीज बताऊंगा"; the dhaba video says "इतने में तो मैं भी ढाबा खोल लूं" then "नहीं खोलना". Curiosity and dread make a
viewer start; a promised personal gain keeps them to the end. A hook with only a mystery and a person in danger is half a hook.

**Every winning hook has**: a concrete scene in sentence one (time, place, a person, a thing you could photograph); a
contradiction between what is seen and what is true; rupees or a life at stake; a question the viewer now needs answered; and the
promise of ONE specific reveal (a small thing, one number, one mistake, one share-taker), often "जवाब आखिर में". No greeting, no
channel intro, no subscribe ask (the two flops with weak hooks both ask in the first 30 s and open with analysis, not a scene).

## 5. Re-hooks: the mechanisms, and what makes one real

A re-hook works only when it **promises something specific** or **raises the danger to someone we care about**. A generic line
("लेकिन कहानी यहीं खत्म नहीं होती", "आगे जो हुआ वो चौंकाने वाला था") promises nothing and is heard as filler.

| Mechanism | Real line | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Announce the next turn just before it | "सब कुछ बहुत अच्छा चल रहा था कि तभी 1944 में उनकी लाइफ में एक बहुत बड़ा ट्विस्ट आया" | the calm line is itself the alarm |
| Foreshadow tied to a plant | "11 महीने बाद रात के 2:40 पर यह बात रघु को याद आने वाली थी" | we know the fire is coming; now we know this line matters |
| Defer the exact answer | "उस टिकट में 10 हिस्सेदार हैं। मालिक की बारी 11वीं। जवाब आखिर में।" | a countable promise; the viewer keeps score |
| Challenge the viewer's math | "आप भी यही सोच रहे हैं ना? याद रखिए, यह हिसाब आखिर में पलटेगा।" | they just did the math; now it is threatened |
| A share-taker walks in | "फिर बाकी हिस्सेदार भी कतार में आ खड़े हुए" | the count goes up; how much is left? |
| The mentor's riddle | "तेरे यहां दिक्कत खाने की नहीं है। तेरे यहां दिक्कत कचरे की है।" | an answer that is itself a question |
| Invite a prediction | "आपको क्या लगता है? ग्राहक भाग गए होंगे। रुकिए, आगे सुनिए।" | the viewer bets, then must stay to see |
| Escalation rung | "लेकिन असली धमाका अब हुआ।" | the stakes just went up a level |
| The threat | "मुंडे साहब बहुत तेज दौड़ रहे हैं आप... घर परिवार का ख्याल रखिए।" | danger to the hero's family |
| The cold open catches up | "घड़ी में बजे थे 2:40 और रघु का फोन बजा" | the dread we carried since minute one arrives |
| Insider secret | "अब वो हिस्सा जो लगभग किसी को नहीं पता" | it must then deliver something genuinely non-obvious |
| Pay a plant | "जिस छोटी चीज की मैंने शुरू में बात की थी, वह है पापड़ और छाछ" | the click of a promise kept buys trust for the next one |

## 6. How a scene is written (the texture)

- **Present tense, short sentences in action; time, place, sound, light, one object**: "लाल बत्ती जली, घंटी चीखी, शीशे में सफेद
  धुआं।" "अंधेरी सड़क पर टॉर्च दांतों में दबाकर रघु ने खुद टायर बदला।" "रघु चाय की टपरी पर बैठा। जमीन से एक पुराना टिकट उठाया।"
- **Dialogue is short and does work**: it humiliates, threatens, teaches or reveals. "बस रुक सकती है। किस्त नहीं रुकती।" "पनीर बेचकर
  वाहवाही मिलती है। दाल बेचकर पैसा मिलता है।" Real people only say their documented words; invented characters may talk.
- **Silence and reaction instead of adjectives**: "अड्डे पर सन्नाटा छा गया।" "सरला कुछ नहीं बोली। पर देर रात तक लोहे की अलमारी के पास
  बैठी रही।" "राजू चौंका: कचरा?" (Not "उसकी सांस एक पल को रुक जाती है", which tells the feeling instead of showing a cause.)
- **Numbers arrive as consequences**: the profit is ₹7,000 *because* he printed 62 items and the dustbin ate ₹75,000. A number with no
  event behind it is a lecture.
- **Humour comes from the character's mistakes**: "ढाबा कम, पूरा शादी का केटरर लग रहा था।"
- **One image per chapter that sticks**: "एक बस जो खड़ी है वह एक घाव है जो हर दिन गहरा होता जाता है।"
- **Aphorisms close a beat**: "छापा एक दुकान बंद करता है। लेकिन जानकारी हर ग्राहक को ताकत देती है।"
- **Register by channel**: Shivanshu plain, fast, past tense, Hinglish business words; HiddenOps cinematic present tense with
  dialogue; Money Code and HinExp conversational "आप" with "भाई", "जी हां", "अब सोचिए"; Paisa Decode "तुम" like a friend.
  Decoding Vyapaar: HiddenOps' cinema for story beats, Money Code's warmth for the explaining beats, "आप".

## 7. Closes

1. **The answer, staged**: the hook's question answered with the exact number as a countdown the viewer can follow ("10 हिस्सेदार
   अपना-अपना ले गए, 11वें के हाथ में आए ₹20").
2. **The irony that calls back the wound**: "कैलाश सेठ ने कहा था तू ड्राइवर, मैं सेठ। 5 साल सेठ बनकर रघु ने कमाया अपने ड्राइवर से 1
   लाख कम।"
3. **One last turn**, usually upward ("पर अब कहानी पलटती है। किस्तें खत्म...") or a chilling one ("अपराध हमेशा डरावने चेहरे के साथ
   नहीं आता। कभी-कभी वह सफेद होता है, दूध जैसा।").
4. **A last image back in the opening scene** (the milk tank at 2 am; the DMart billing queue next Sunday; outside the same loan
   office with papers for a second bus).
5. **The maxim** ("बिक्री आपको बड़ा दिखा सकती है, लेकिन कंट्रोल आपको टिकाऊ बनाता है") and **the observation test** ("अगली बार जब आप
   उस लाइन में खड़े हों, तो एक बार आसपास देख लीजिएगा कितने लोग सामान गिन रहे हैं और कितने लोग फोन").
6. **One specific comment question**, two-sided or personal ("आपके घर की पहली गाड़ी कौन सी थी?"), then the ask, the next video
   named, the sign-off.

## 8. The anti-example: why our first jewellery script was flat

Chapters were cost categories ("Investment: Gold in the Safe", "Revenue: One Necklace Bill", "The Expenses Nobody Sees"): a lecture
with a story painted on. Ramesh had no pain, made no mistake, lost nothing and wanted nothing; Ankit was an MBA mouthpiece who
explained. The hook asked a question but put no one in danger and nothing in the viewer's hand. Re-hooks were stock phrases ("लेकिन
कहानी यहीं खत्म नहीं होती"). Feelings were told ("चेहरे पर मुस्कान आ जाती है", "अंकित की सांस एक पल को रुक जाती है") instead of
caused. Sources were read aloud ("World Gold Council के मुताबिक", "CRISIL के मुताबिक") like a research note. It passed every count
in the old checker, which is the clearest proof that the counts were never the craft.
