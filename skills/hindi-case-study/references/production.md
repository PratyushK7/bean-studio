# Production: from an approved script to a finished cut

The script skill stops at Gate A, where the owner approves `<slug>-script.md` and the free listen. This file is the visual
production system that follows it. It was rebuilt in November 2026 so the channel looks like an art-directed documentary,
not generic AI pictures with automatic zooms.

**One source of truth:** `C:\Users\praty\youtube\channels\hindi\visual-bible.json`. It holds the style, the palette, the
lighting looks, the shot vocabulary, the composition rules, the negatives, the character rules, the graphics design system
and every render setting. The readable copy is `visual-bible.md`, generated by `python tools/vyapaar_visual.py bible`. A
project may override any key in `<project>/visual-bible.json`.

A Haiku child never approves, rejects or rewrites anything: it reports, and the decision comes back to Opus.

Learned on the first run (16 Nov 2026):
- **Fully automatic jobs need no model at all.** The runner, the upscale and the render cost nothing while they run, so
  run them as background processes and spend no tokens waiting. A child that ends its turn while its job runs can wake
  later and act on stale assumptions; it closed a tab it believed was its own.
- **Haiku is for short multi-step chores** that need light judgment, such as re-running a failed file or tidying tabs.
- **Pilot before the batch.** Make the character sheets first and approve them. Then make about 6 deliberately
  different scenes (no people, night, sun, cool showroom light) and judge them before releasing the full batch.
- **Style drift.** "Like a painted documentary film still" produced near-photographs, all rejected. The bible's style words
  now say "an illustration and not a photograph, gentle cel-like faces". New sheets attach an approved sheet as a style
  reference only (`style_ref` in the plan), and the runner does not tell ChatGPT to copy that person.

Legacy, kept for old projects and for rollback:
- `vyapaar_prompts.py --legacy` (the one-style-string prompts)
- `vyapaar_assemble.py` (the 48 fps cutter with random reframing)
- `vyapaar_styles.py` (the style experiments A to D and K: K's settings now live in the bible)

## 1. The visual plan (the art director's job)

Write `visual-plan.json` from the engine and the beat sheet, never by regex from the visual notes.

- **Characters.** One entry per recurring person:
  - name, age, face, skin, hair, body, clothing, accessories, features
  - `identity`, the words that describe the face
  - `short`: three to five identity details, used when the reference sheet is attached
  - `sheet`: the approved reference picture number, or nothing to make one
  - Anyone who appears more than once gets a sheet. An approved sheet beats a later script note. The validator lists every
    note it overrides.
- **Locations.** `prompt` (what the place looks like, materials named), `short` (a one-line hint for tight shots), and `look`
  (its default light).
- **Shots.** One per picture or graphic. Use the fields that matter for that shot:
  - `size` (establishing, wide, medium, medium_close, close, extreme_close, insert, macro, ots, pov, reaction, two_shot) and
    `angle`
  - `look` (the light follows the story's clock: festival night, morning, the ledger nights, midnight, the flashback)
  - `chars` {id: expression and body}: never a default smile. "Only his hand" makes a hand-only shot with no face words.
  - `action`: the exact moment the line describes
  - `fg` / `mg` / `bg`, `placement`, `light`, `props`, `continuity`, `costume`, `avoid`, `crowd`, `flashback`
  - `candidates: 2` for the shots that carry the story
  - `same_as` for a callback that reuses an earlier picture
  - `reuse` for an existing picture number
  - `camera` for a planned move
  - `motion` for an image-to-video brief
- **Graphics.** Cards are `kind: graphic` with a `graphic` spec and its `facts` ids. The types are number, bill, stack,
  bars, change, line, share, flow, title and triad. Every number must trace to a research row or the line's screen text, or
  the validator fails it; a deliberate floor ("more than ₹1 crore") goes in `allow` with `allow_reason`.
- **Never ask the image model for a diagram or for text.** Maps with icons, see-saws and tickers are graphics. Bills, apps
  and boards are shown illegible, as real objects.

## 2. Pictures and Gate 1

Make and approve the character sheets first. Then make the scene files. The extension cannot attach sheets by itself:
`prompts/REFS.md` lists the exact files for every prompt, and the runner attaches them from `refs`.

What `vyapaar_review.py` checks by machine:
- resolution and aspect
- brightness and saturation against the shot's planned look; this caught v2's midnight shots lit like noon
- blown highlights and blur
- near-duplicate pictures (visual repetition)
- face counts
- a stale prompt (the plan changed after the picture was made)
- two faces that look like the same person (SFace identity vectors, OpenCV's model): a duplicated person, a look-alike extra
  or a split frame. It is a heuristic: on the v2 set, 4 of its 7 flags were real; the rest were two different older men who
  look alike in this style.
- a weak likeness to the character's sheet, as a note only: the model cannot reliably tell two middle-aged men apart, so who
  is who stays a human check

What a person checks on the contact sheets (`review/*.jpg`, grouped into sheets, establishing, characters, close-ups and
graphics, with candidates side by side): the right person, hands and faces, composition, light against the look,
environment realism, the picture against the line, the style, and continuity with the shots before and after.

How to act on a picture:
- `reject` moves the picture to `images/_rejected/`, and only that slot goes into the next prompt file.
- `pick` swaps in candidate b.
- Upscale only after approval. An upscaled bad picture is still a bad picture.

## 3. Clips (optional, paid)

`select` scores shots for the story (hook, reveals, payoffs, important shots, a written `motion` brief) against what
image-to-video does badly (crowds morph, hands warp, papers and screens grow text). It keeps 45 s between clips and stays
within `--budget`.

Each brief has the same parts:
- where the shot starts and ends
- one or two things that move
- the camera and the intensity
- what must stay still
- identity and style preservation

`make` needs `--approve-cost`, the picture must be approved in Gate 1, and the files are `clips/S###.mp4`, keyed by shot,
because line numbers change between script versions. `check` measures flicker, jumps, identity drift and softening, and
writes 8-frame strips; a person approves every clip. An approval records a fingerprint of the line, so a clip approved for
another version of the line is never used.

**Frame rate, measured** (`fps-test`, on the Gupta ji clip): motion interpolation to 30 fps lowered median sharpness to 93.5%
of the source and made 2 frames soft, while evening the motion by almost nothing (cadence 0.449 against 0.452). The old 48 fps
minterpolate step was a loss. Clips stay at their native 24 fps and are conformed by frame repetition.

## 4. Graphics

One HTML template set (`tools/video/graphics.html`), rendered frame by frame in Chromium, which shapes Devanagari correctly;
this machine's Pillow cannot.
- **One design system.** The bible's tokens set Nirmala UI for text, Bahnschrift for numbers, warm dark panels, restrained
  gold, loss red and gain green, one motion curve and safe margins.
- **Timing.** Elements enter in the order the narration says them (a `cues` word places an element on that word). A slow
  2.5% push keeps a finished graphic from freezing.
- **Recurring motifs.** One graphic idea recurs through a video: the necklace bill fills up, line by line, until the six
  names.
- **QA in the page.** It reports text outside its box, outside the safe area, or overlapping other text; G048's long values
  were caught this way, and long values now shrink to fit.
- **Lower thirds.** Still lines with `screen` text get a lower-third label, faded in and out by the renderer.

## 5. The renderer and Gate 2

`vyapaar_render.py` is the only renderer. The sample and the full cut share every code path and every setting, all taken
from the bible.

**Camera.** A move is chosen from the picture's measured composition (faces and subject mask) and the shot's size and
purpose, or taken from the plan's `camera`:
- push to the subject
- pull back
- drift across
- pan from face to face
- a near-hold for inserts

The same move never runs twice in a row. Speed is capped at 0.02 zoom and 0.028 frame-widths a second, and moves ease in and
out. A line over 7 s gets one motivated cut-in to the subject, but only when the upscaled source has the pixels for it.

**Transitions.** A cut, unless the location changes (0.4 s dissolve), a chapter starts (0.6 s) or a flashback begins or ends
(0.9 s, plus the bible's flashback look).

**Grade.** One grade, in the encoder, on stills, graphics and clips alike: slightly warm shadows, saturation down so gold
never floods, a light vignette and fine grain. It never hides a bad picture; Gate 1 rejects those.

**Audio.** Voice EQ from the bible, plus real music from `<project>/audio/music-map.json` following the script's moods,
ducked under the voice and levelled to -14 LUFS.

**A full render refuses to start** unless Gate 1 is open and Gate 2 approved a sample whose settings hash matches. If
anything in the bible's render or graphics changes, a new sample must be approved.

`vyapaar_qa.py` looks at the video, not at ffmpeg's exit code:
- frozen stretches, black frames, repeated frames and unplanned jumps
- frame rate, resolution, A/V length, loudness and true peak
- camera speed against the limits, repeated moves, pushes a minute, and a picture reused without a new framing
- brightness and saturation per shot, measured on the video, against the look and the neighbours
- text clipping in graphics
- unreviewed clips

It writes a one-frame-per-shot sheet and strips of the opening, the middle and the ending to look at.
