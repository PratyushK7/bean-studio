# Chapter architecture: choosing and ordering the story

Chapters are chosen for **storytelling potential**, not because material exists or because it comes next in the calendar.
RZ never tells the childhood after minute one; AG puts a mid-life miracle first and the birth fourth; SM opens with a
stranger's story and only then starts the life. Chronology is the fallback, not the plan.

All of this goes in `<slug>-plan.md` before a single narration line is written.

## Step 1. The central question

One question the whole video answers. It must be:
- **genuinely open** to the viewer (they don't already know the answer),
- **answerable honestly** from the ledger (even if the honest answer is "history can't decide this; here is what it shows"),
- **about a person, event or place**, phrased the way a viewer would ask it.

Write it, the honest answer (two sentences), and the takeaway (one sentence the viewer repeats to a friend).

Good: "क्या नीम करोली बाबा सच में हनुमान जी थे?" / "एक साधु के पास दुनिया के सबसे बड़े टेक-फ़ाउंडर्स क्यों गए?"
Weak: "नीम करोली बाबा का जीवन" (a topic, not a question).

## Step 2. The story inventory and its scores

List every candidate anecdote, person, place and fact-cluster from the ledger. Score each 0 to 5 on six measures:

| Measure | 5 means |
|---|---|
| **Surprise** | the viewer will not see the reveal coming |
| **Stakes** | life, death, a family, a fortune, a reputation |
| **Specificity** | the sources give concrete details (objects, numbers, places, words) for the engine's ten steps |
| **Emotion** | it moves: fear, grief, relief, awe, laughter |
| **Connection** | it links to something the viewer already knows (a famous name, a place they visit, their own life) |
| **Relevance** | it moves the central question |

Then note the **evidence strength** separately (the best ledger status among its claims). Evidence does not lower the story
score; it decides **how the story is told** (attribution, vagueness where versions conflict) and whether it can be told at
all (`unsupported` cannot).

Keep stories scoring 18+ for full anecdotes; 12 to 17 become vignettes or montage lines; under 12 are cut unless the
structure needs them (a date, a turn).

## Step 3. The opening

Choose the cold open from the top of the inventory using these tests, in order:
1. **Self-contained**: it makes sense with no background.
2. **Visual**: a viewer can picture it (a train that won't move, soap on a face, a professor who doubts).
3. **Ends on a reveal or a contradiction** that points at the central question.
4. **Has a callback image** the ending can return to with a new meaning.
5. **Not the single best story** in a Standard or Long video: hold the best for the 60 to 75% mark. In a Short video the
   strongest clean story can open.

Types that work (from the references): an outsider's encounter (SM), a staged miracle (AG), a compressed origin with a
turn (RZ).

## Step 4. Sequencing the revelations

Place the inventory on the runtime template (`runtime-templates.md`) with these rules:

- **Escalate by stakes:** personal → life and death → public scale.
- **Alternate textures:** story, explanation, story, testimony, consequence. Never two explainers back to back.
- **Hold the identity reveals:** famous names, the true identity of a stranger, a number that changes the meaning.
- **Seed early, pay late:** a maxim, an object or a promise planted in the first third pays off in the last half (AG's
  "पहले खिलाओ" at 07:23, paid at 24:45).
- **The second-best story at the end of the first third, the best at 60 to 75%.**
- **Emotional low before the resolution:** a loss or near-miss in the last quarter makes the payoff land.
- **Chronology inside chapters, not across the video:** within a chapter, keep time moving forward; across chapters, order by
  pull. Re-orient with a date stamp whenever time jumps.

## Step 5. What to withhold

List, explicitly, what the viewer must **not** learn early:

| Withheld | Revealed at | Why it waits |
|---|---|---|
| the name of the famous visitor | the end of his story | delayed reveal (D4) |
| what the blanket held | after the son's account | withheld object (D10) |
| the honest answer to the central question | the resolution | the video's promise |

## Step 6. The loop table

Every open question is a loop. The table proves each one closes.

| Loop | Opened at (line / time) | Opened by | Closed at | Closed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 (central question) | 0:55 | "क्या ...?" | 19:30 | the honest answer |
| L1 | 1:48 | "तो फिर यह बात उन्हें कैसे पता चली?" | 2:30 | no explanation exists; the believers' reading |

Rules: the central loop stays open all video; other loops close within a third of the runtime; at most 2 / 3 / 4 open at
once (Short / Standard / Long); a loop opened by a "but" closes within 60 s; a mention of a story ("कंबल से जुड़ी कई कहानियाँ
हैं ...") is a loop, so tell it or cut the mention (SM 09:47).

## Step 7. The chapter cards

One block per chapter in the plan:

```
Ch 3 · कैंची: एक मोड़ जहाँ कुछ नहीं था (9:00 to 11:30, ~400 words)
Question it answers: why did people start travelling to this spot?
Opens with: CH4 (place), date stamp 1962
Anecdotes: A5 (founding, vignette), A6 (bhandara, full)
New things learned: the name's meaning; who was there before; the scale today
Turn: from an empty bend to a crowd of a lakh
Re-hooks: 9:40 (MS1), 10:50 (T2 foreshadow into the outsiders)
Loops: opens L4, closes L2
Facts: F4, F27, AG16, SM29
Handoff line (last sentence): a foreshadow of the first outsider
Emotion: curiosity → awe
```

Chapter titles on screen name an event or place in 3 to 6 words; never "भाग 3".

## Step 8. Handoffs

The last sentence of every chapter makes the next chapter necessary. It is one of: a foreshadow (T2), a time jump promise,
a question the next chapter answers, a consequence not yet explained, or a person introduced but not yet met. Never a
summary.

## Step 9. The emotional map

Plot the intended feeling per chapter (curious, uneasy, amused, moved, awed, reflective). Two chapters in a row with the
same feeling need a change of texture inside them. The last 10% moves from reflective to warm or resolved, never to fear.

## Example (Neem Karoli Baba, Standard band, from the reference ledger)

| Slot | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
