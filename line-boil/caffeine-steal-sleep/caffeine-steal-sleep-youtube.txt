== DESCRIPTION ==
How does caffeine affect sleep, and how long does caffeine stay in your system? Caffeine doesn't give you energy: it blocks the signal that says you're tired.
Here's why a 3 p.m. coffee can still cost you sleep at midnight, told with cartoon beans and checked against the studies.

The short answer: while you're awake, a molecule called adenosine builds up and tells your brain you're tired. Caffeine sits in adenosine's receptors and blocks the message. Your liver clears it slowly: the half-life is around four to five hours on average in healthy adults, but it ranges from about two hours to eight or more. So with a 5-hour half-life, more than a quarter of a 3 p.m. coffee is still in you at midnight. In a 2013 study, 400 mg of caffeine taken six hours before bed still cut more than an hour of sleep, and the sleepers' own diaries didn't clearly notice. A 2023 review of 24 studies found caffeine cut about 45 minutes of sleep on average, with less deep sleep.

A general science explainer, not medical advice. If you're pregnant or on medication, ask your doctor about caffeine.
