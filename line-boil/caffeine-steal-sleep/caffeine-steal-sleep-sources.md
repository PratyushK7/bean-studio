# Sources: How Does Caffeine Steal Your Sleep?

Every claim in `script.json` that carries a fact, the source behind it, and how far it was checked. Line numbers are `script.json` indices (from 0).
"Full text" = read in the paper or page itself; "abstract" = abstract or agency summary only; "secondary" = only via a report citing it.

| 1 | Adenosine builds up in the brain the longer you are awake, slows wake-promoting (cholinergic basal forebrain) cells, and falls during sleep (lines 12-22) | Porkka-Heiskanen T. et al. ## Not claimed, on purpose
- No "caffeine crash" mechanism beyond "the tiredness is put on hold": the letters piling up stands for adenosine continuing to build while you are awake (source 1), not a claim that caffeine raises adenosine.
- No numbers for genetics (CYP1A2 variants), pregnancy or particular medicines: the brief said not to overclaim, and the script points to a doctor.
- No tolerance or receptor up-regulation claims (the evidence is mostly animal work).
- No personal cut-off advice beyond reporting the meta-analysis authors' average suggestion, labelled as an average.
