== DESCRIPTION ==
Why do prices end in .99? Because your brain reads a price left to right and leans on the first digit, so $3.99 feels like a three, not a four.
A shopkeeper, one cent and a lamp explain the left-digit effect in 40 seconds.

The short answer: studies of shoppers find that prices just under a round number tend to sell more than the one-cent difference would explain, probably because people lean on the leftmost digit. The rule to take away: read the left digit, then ask what it is really worth. (The lamp story is an illustration, not data.)
